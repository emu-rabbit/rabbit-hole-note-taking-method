# Make curiosity easy to follow

Help a reader move across stories, people, periods, and works without understanding the folder tree or reading the entire vault.

Follow the [shared conventions](../rabbit-hole-start/references/conventions.md), including first-use orientation to the author’s method. Read the vault’s guidance and the relevant source, index, and progress notes for this task. Read the actual pages in scope and identify their existing IDs, headings, and passage anchors. Verify a connection against cited sources when the notes alone leave it uncertain.

## Give each entrance a job

README welcomes the reader and offers entry choices. The profile introduces the person. A reading map curates journeys; an index supports lookup. A timeline organizes chronology, and a relationship map introduces established people and circles. Each leads to the fuller account rather than reproducing it. Begin with the existing small index and add specialized indexes when they improve finding material.

## Build and walk a route

1. Choose a question or scene the available pages can answer. A route may be one rich story or a few stops. At the entrance, offer a plain-language invitation and show the route’s extent, naming a section when it ends partway through a long page.
2. Link meaningful transitions: a scene to someone in it, a work to its making, a period to an experience. Explain when two scenes are simply worth reading together, keeping that distinct from a causal or biographical claim.
3. Put reader choices in the entrance and brief next-step/return navigation on the actual pages. Let each article’s prose end naturally. Preserve routes already using those pages and make deeper source detours visibly optional.
4. Update the affected lookup entries and use natural first-mention links in prose. Reuse existing identities; leave an undeveloped idea as a maintenance question until it has a real target.
5. Follow every changed route from entrance to stopping point, including the return path and source anchors. Run the shared checker and repair navigation that is structurally valid but confusing to read.

## Done means

The reader can find the requested material, understand why the route moves between pages, and stop without unfinished mandatory branches. Return the entrance, suggested route, changed files, and remaining gaps, with the scope of the pass clear.
