# Write a person readers can come to know

Make room for daily life, companionship, unfinished attempts, and changing perspectives alongside achievements. Let the available experience determine the shape and ending.

Follow the [shared conventions](../rabbit-hole-start/references/conventions.md), including first-use orientation to the author’s method. Read the vault’s guidance and the relevant source, index, and progress notes for this task. Read the current target page and complete relevant source passages. Identify the evidence for proposed facts and reported feelings, the voices and dates involved, and existing identities for the people, stories, and works. A small pass can use one source and produce one useful note.

## Choose the page that owns the detail

- **Story:** unfold the scene, introducing people and circumstances naturally at first appearance.
- **Person/profile:** introduce who the person is, their documented life and qualities, then follow the relationship over time.
- **Life period or timeline:** place experiences, relationships, daily life, and work in their supported chronology.
- **Life topic:** begin with a supported understanding and develop it through particular scenes across time.
- **Creative work:** explain what it is, why it was made, its development, and each contributor’s actual role. Link the full making-of scene to a story.
- **Favorite:** preserve what the person enjoys and why, with the period and expressed feelings that give it context.

Keep a short account together when that serves the reader. Other pages can offer brief, independently understandable summaries and links to the full account.

## Draft, then read as a newcomer

1. Write in third person by default, honoring the owner’s chosen voice. Use concrete details the evidence supports. Attribute remembered feelings and interpretations to their speaker and time; preserve unresolved differences between accounts.
2. Open with the work, people, and circumstances a newcomer needs to enter the scene, then let the narrative follow what happened. Keep material uncertainty visible beside the relevant claim; place detailed date/source reconciliation in footnotes or maintenance records. Short pieces can flow without headings; longer pieces benefit from headings that describe their actual content.
3. Place footnotes near supported claims, linking to exact existing source passages. Cite each relevant source when combining accounts. Add natural first-mention links to established people, stories, or works.
4. Compare the wording with its sources, preserving who did what for whom, whether it happened, was intended, or was promised, and the source’s date precision; then read as someone meeting this person for the first time. Check that the scene is understandable, the person has context, and transitions carry only supported meaning. Revise prose that feels like a record dump, an unsupported character judgment, or a forced life lesson.
5. For an authorized saved-note pass, update affected indexes and reading coverage, and record substantive changes of understanding with their evidence. For a chat draft, report these in the response. Apply the relevant structural and editorial checks.

## Done means

A saved note, or a chat draft when only drafting was requested, reads independently and lets the reader return to the evidence. Report paths, source coverage, important uncertainty, and check results. Leave unknown details open rather than filling them to complete a scene.
