# Interview through a scene

Read the existing entrance and relevant source/progress notes before choosing a question. Begin from the owner's chosen topic or a small everyday moment; existing records can invite a memory without setting its interpretation.

Ask one or two comfortable questions, then wait. Follow the answer through setting, sequence, companionship and details that mattered to the speaker. Feelings then and understanding now may differ. Keep a new thread as an optional later question rather than turning the conversation into a questionnaire.

When referring to a source, give its date, a brief exact excerpt and locator. Preserve whether an event happened, was planned, or was remembered later. Clarify identity or time only when it changes the understanding; an approximate date or “I don't remember” can remain usable evidence.

At a natural stopping point, honor the person's pace and the agreed saving scope. Keep off-record material out of files, summaries and progress notes. A conversation-only account stays in the chat, with files unchanged.

For a saved account, preserve narrator, narration date, event date or uncertainty, source type and stable passage locators. Keep questions, answers, quoted speech and editorial summaries distinguishable. Use an accurately labeled edited account if exact conversation wording is unavailable. Continue through the shared saved-work workflow so the memory becomes part of the rabbit hole without another request.
