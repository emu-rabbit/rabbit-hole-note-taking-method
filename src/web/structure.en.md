## 00_Entry: Find your way in

This section offers four places to start. The profile introduces you as a person. The reading map suggests routes through stories, people, periods, and creative work. The index explains what each section holds and where to look. The method explains how to document a person and what principles guide that work.

## 10_Life timeline: Get to know you over time

Write a note for each stage of your life. Bring together your daily life, relationships, work, and creative projects from that period so readers can see the circumstances you were in. The timeline puts events in order and links to relevant periods, people, stories, and work. Readers can follow it from one stage to the next or look up when something happened.

## 20_Self and daily life: See what carries across different periods

Some parts of your life come up again and again, such as learning, names and how you present yourself, work, everyday interests, or what you want from love and relationships. Give each topic its own note. Use scenes from different years to show what carried through and what changed, and link them back to specific stories and people.

## 30_Stories: Step into a complete scene

The story index gives each story a time period and a one-line description so readers can choose a scene to enter. Keep the stories in `故事篇章/`. A trip, a goodbye, a time someone cared for you, or the first attempt at making something can all become stories. Keep the setting, what happened, and how it felt together.

## 40_Important people: Understand relationships through the people in them

This section focuses on relationships formed through spending time together in real life. The relationship map leads to the people and circles that matter to you.

- `人物/` holds individual profiles. Introduce the person, their personality, and their life, then follow how you spent time together and how your feelings and relationship changed.
- `關係圈/` records groups formed through living, studying, working, or taking part in activities together. It explains how each group became part of your life.

Keep a person's changing role and closeness to you in the same note, and link it to the stories you share.

## 50_Creative work: Keep what you made and why you made it

The creative work index groups projects by how you took part and leads to the corresponding notes and folders.

- `自主創作/` holds work you led. Record what motivated you, what you made, how it took shape and changed, and what collaborators contributed.
- `委託與共同創作/` holds commissions, concepts you helped develop, and collaborations. Make each person's role clear.

The work page introduces the piece itself. Link to a story for the full scene of making it. Writing, images, crafts, performances, tools, and unfinished attempts all have a place here.

## 60_Favorites: Remember what you enjoy and admire

Games, music, books, films, toys, activities, and people you admire can all have a place here. Write what you like, which parts you like, and how they made you feel at the time. The index lists the categories and entries. Keep each entry in its category folder. A short entry can become a longer note when there is more to say.

Choose categories that fit your material, such as music, performers, games, films, animation, or objects. Add a category when you have something to put in it, and let the categories change as your interests and notes grow.

This section is for the people and things you like. Relationships formed through spending time together belong in Important people. Experiences with more complicated feelings can live in the relevant life topic or story. Link between the sections when they overlap so the full account has a place of its own.

## 80_Sources: Return to the words from that time

Footnotes lead here, and the source index is the starting point. Keep original wording, dates, and passages readers can locate so they can hear the voices from that time and see what later interpretations are based on.

Organize the source folders according to the material you have and are willing to share and authorize for use. Names can reflect the type of material, how it was obtained, or who provided it.

- Your accounts include interviews, memories, and personal narratives. Record when the account was given to fill in parts of life and feelings absent from existing records.
- Records from the time include diaries, public posts, and other saved material. Keep their original dates, sources, and context.
- Material about creative work includes ideas, drafts, introductions, and revision history. Connect it to the circumstances in which the work took shape.
- Review records document what was consulted, why it was available for review, and what could be read, so a later editor can check it again.

## 90_Maintenance: Help the next person pick up where you left off

This section helps you, another editor, or an AI agent continue the work. The maintenance guide covers adding interviews back into the notes, writing conventions, checking indexes, and reviewing and saving changes. `理解更新紀錄.md` records why the understanding of you changed. `訪談路線.md` records what has been discussed and what questions can come next. `資料與閱讀進度.md` tracks the material covered and how far the close reading has gone. Evidence indexes, check reports, and import checks make it possible to trace the basis of edits and their review results.

Adjust the files and tools in the maintenance section to your needs. They help you keep the rabbit hole accurate and make it easier to continue the work.
