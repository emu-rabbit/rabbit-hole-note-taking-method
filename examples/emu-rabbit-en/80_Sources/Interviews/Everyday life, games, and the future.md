# Everyday life, games, and the future

English translations of the published Chinese interview excerpts. These quotations are translations, not verbatim English originals. […] marks omissions; pauses are retained. Introductions identify the topic and are not verbatim quotations.

## Mengmeng's everyday life and the towel

Source: excerpts from an interview with the author.

> I would let her out to run around the room. She wasn't affectionate with me, but she loved exploring. I'd watch her run around while keeping an eye on her safety.

^everyday-mengmeng-exploring

> […]just eating, then sleeping. Then when I went to sleep at night, she'd get up and run on her wheel and make noise.

^everyday-mengmeng-wheel

> The picture in nAnB was one I happened to take one day. She was sitting in a corner of the cage, with her back to me, looking at the wall, a bit like a world-weary middle-aged man. I found it funny and really liked the picture.

^everyday-mengmeng-picture

> When I took Mengmeng out in her carrier, I'd cover it with a towel because I was worried about the sun. She chewed that towel to shreds. In fact, so many years have passed, and I still use that towel to dry my hair.

^everyday-mengmeng

## Minecraft: production lines and a particle collider

Source: excerpts from an interview with the author. She recalled that the collider's difficulty was getting particles to move from the small ring to the large ring. The excerpts also preserve her answers about building and what happened after she finished.

> How do I like to play.....probably factories. I like automating all the production lines, and the pipes all have to be laid out beautifully. They even need facades to disguise them as floors and walls. Because Minecraft is a block game, symmetry, even spacing and a consistent look are all things I care about when laying out the pipes.

^everyday-minecraft-pipes

> I spent a long time building this, and at the end I redid the whole thing to make it beautiful.

^everyday-minecraft-building

On the difficulty of moving particles to the large ring:

> […]playing with mods means you really, really, really, really need to deal with wikis and tutorial sites and things like that. I failed so many times along the way that I was nearly going crazy.

^everyday-minecraft-rings

Asked how she celebrated succeeding:

> I didn't, really. I just kept playing, haha.

^everyday-minecraft-playing

She later added:

> There really is a sense of achievement. You'll notice that I spend a large part of this video just wandering around the plaza.

^everyday-minecraft

## Seeing a future before thinking of saving

Source: an excerpt from an interview with the author. She no longer remembers how she got started, but corrected the order of events:

> […]it was having a future in sight that made me think, oh, I should start saving, and then learn to manage money.

^everyday-future

## Childhood mathematics and enjoying it again later

Source: excerpts from an interview with the author, all later recollections. The childhood assignments involved drawing a clock showing four forty and waiting in line for the teacher to mark written arithmetic. The exact school year and calendar year have not been established.

> […]for example, four…forty, please draw it on a clock[…]

^everyday-mathematics-task

> The hour hand I drew…wasn't right on the four. It went out toward five, and even had a sense of proportion, about two-thirds of the way between four and five.

^everyday-mathematics-hour-hand

> The teacher would see it and praise me. That really brought out, oh, I seem to be reasonably good at mathematics, able to understand it and do the homework well.

^everyday-mathematics-praise

> […]hey, this part might need borrowing. […]After the teacher finished marking it, I was right! …That made me quite happy too.

^everyday-mathematics-borrowing

> […]even now that I've started learning about personal finance, my…fondness for mathematics has come back, and I'm happy, because it can help me. It's a very useful tool.

^everyday-mathematics

## Being recognized through the Workshop and jokes about rabbit meat

Source: excerpts from an interview with the author, recalling players' responses to her work.

> In my mind, the crafting tool really is a well-known child. Very outgoing, very well liked. After all, it solves so many players' problems, so naturally people like it.

^everyday-workshop-child

> […]putting my own player ID in the website title meant that afterward people would often recognize me in the game. Everyone praised my work, which made me really happy and satisfied my wish to be seen.

^everyday-workshop-recognition

> At the same time, frozen rabbit meat that couldn't actually be eaten became the joke people most often brought up with me. Some wanted to roast it, some wanted to microwave it, […]calling it three-cup rabbit. The little talking point planted when the Workshop began grew and had an effect.

^everyday-workshop
